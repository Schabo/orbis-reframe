# DJI 360 cameras file format — `.osv`

**Desk research (2026-10-15); one Avata 360 clip examined 2026-11-14.**
The desk research comes from public specs and Gyroflow's telemetry-parser
(`src/dji/`, MIT OR Apache-2.0), which ships the protobuf schemas the
cameras write. The rules in [`README.md`](README.md) on what may be taken
from others' reverse engineering apply here in particular, because
telemetry-parser's DJI stitching code partly cites DJI Studio's internals.
Markers as in [`README.md`](README.md): ✅ is checked on our one Avata 360
clip (*Modes and sample clips*); nothing about the Osmo 360 family has met
a real file yet.

Covers the DJI Osmo 360 (2025), Osmo 360 II, Osmo 360 Air and the Avata 360
drone. The app doesn't open `.OSV` files yet.

## 1. Files and streams

- 📖 360 video is recorded as `.OSV`; single-lens and flat modes as `.MP4`.
  Final Cut Pro doesn't open `.OSV` directly (DJI Studio exports MP4).
- 📖 Osmo 360: two square ~1" sensors, f/1.9. 360 video up to 8K 50
  (8K 30 for long takes), 6K, and 4K 100. **10-bit is available in every
  mode**, with D-Log M as a log profile. ❓ Codec, presumably H.265;
  ❓ whether 8-bit stays the default.
- ✅ A take is three files with one name, in upper case: the `.OSV`, a
  `.LRF` proxy and a `.SRT` subtitle file. The camera names them
  `DJI_<YYYYMMDDhhmmss>_<NNNN>_D_<…>` (on our clip the last part is
  `2026`; ❓ whether that's always the year) in `DCIM/DJI_001/`.
- ✅ The `.OSV` and `.LRF` each keep the full path they were recorded to,
  name included, in a `fsid` box in `moov/udta`. A renamed file can be
  matched with the rest of its take by that name.
- ✅ The container is MP4 (`isom`; tags `encoder=DJI Avata360`,
  `comment=EIS:RS;FOV:Linear;`). On the Avata 360 clip its streams are:

  | # | kind | what |
  |---|---|---|
  | 0, 1 | video `hvc1` | the two lenses, one per track: 1920×1920, 100 fps, HEVC Main 10, `yuv420p10le` |
  | 2, 3 | data `djmd` (handler `CAM meta`) | the metadata, one track per sensor (§2) |
  | 4, 5 | data `dbgi` (`CAM dbgi`) | ~47 Mb/s each, ❓ debug data, not read |
  | 6 | data `camd` (`CAM camd`) | 3 samples, ❓ not read |
  | 7 | video MJPEG 960×540 | a cover picture, flagged `attached_pic`: an already reframed flat view, not a fisheye |

  There's no audio track.
- ✅ The lens tracks are tagged BT.709 (primaries, transfer and matrix),
  limited range, **although the clip is D-Log M**: the tags don't tell log
  from standard. The colour mode is in the metadata (§2).
- 📖 Which track holds the **master** lens (the one at yaw ≈ 0) differs
  by product, per telemetry-parser: track 1 on the Osmo 360, track 0 on the
  Osmo 360 II, Osmo 360 Air and Avata 360. Its source is DJI Studio's
  product table, so we only use what our own files show. ❓ On the Avata
  360 clip our seam measurement doesn't settle it yet (§3).
- ✅ The `.LRF` proxy: one HEVC Main (8-bit) track, 1920×960, 25 fps, with
  **both lenses side by side** (lens track 0 on the left), plus the same
  two `djmd` tracks and a `camd`. It's 6 % of the `.OSV`'s size and
  carries the same calibration, so it's the cheap place to read metadata.
- ✅ The `.SRT` has one entry every 20 ms (**50 Hz**, every second frame of
  the 100 fps clip): frame count, wall-clock time, ISO, shutter, f-number,
  EV, colour temperature, `color_md` (`dlog_m`), metering mode, GPS
  latitude / longitude and relative / absolute altitude. It has **no
  attitude**.

## 2. Metadata track

- ✅ The `djmd` tracks carry **one protobuf sample per video frame** (8552
  samples for 8552 frames). Only the first sample holds `clip_meta` and
  `stream_meta` (~6.7 kB); the others only `frame_meta` (~1.4 kB).
- ✅ The two tracks are one per sensor. Their samples are the same apart
  from the timestamps (16 µs apart), the sensor temperature and the
  subject-tracking data; both carry the calibration.
- 📖 The schema differs by product and is **chosen by product name**, not by
  the `proto_file_name` the clip declares (DJI reuses one schema under
  different declared names):

  | product | schema | `pano_dewarp_params` at `StreamMeta` field |
  |---|---|---|
  | Osmo 360 | `dvtm_oq101.proto` | 6 |
  | Osmo 360 II, Osmo 360 Air, Avata 360 | `dvtm_eagle4_wa530.proto` | 5 |

  Parsing with the wrong one silently drops the calibration (the wire types
  disagree).
- ✅ The Avata 360 clip declares `proto_file_name` `dvtm_AVATA360.proto`
  (library 02.01.20, product 02.00.11) and decodes cleanly with
  `dvtm_eagle4_wa530.proto`. `clip_meta_header` names the product
  `DJI Avata360` (no space) and the firmware.
- ✅ Clip metadata on the Avata 360 clip: `imu_sampling_rate` 4000,
  `sensor_fps` 100.019, `sensor_res` 3840×3840, `LRO_value` 2443,
  `sensor_readout_time` 12312719 (❓ units; as ns that's 12.3 ms, against
  9.4 ms for `LRO_value` in ns × 3840 lines), `eis_status` 1.
- ✅ Stream metadata: `video_stream_meta` 1920×1920, 100 fps, `bit_depth`
  10; `color_mode` **19**, which the schema's `ColorModeType` names
  `COLOR_MODE_DLOG_M` (the `.SRT` agrees). So the colour profile is in the
  file; nothing needs the `.SRT`.
- ✅ Frame metadata, per frame: the camera's exposure (ISO, exposure time
  as a fraction, white balance, EV, `focal_length` 2.34 mm, `f_number` 1.8
  where the `.SRT` says 1.9), the sensor temperature, `sensor_shift_param`
  (❓ EIS data); the fused IMU attitude (§4); and on the drone,
  `drone_frame_meta` at 10 Hz: attitude as Euler angles in 0.1° (NED),
  velocity, GPS, altitude, and `tracking_info` while the drone follows a
  subject.

## 3. Lens calibration

- 📖 `PanoDewarpParams` holds **24 calibrations**, a slave/master pair for
  each of 12 cases: native, native-refined, refined at infinity ("far"),
  refined at stated distances (`far_07` … `far_16`), lens guards, and the
  waterproof case above and under water.
- ✅ On the Avata 360 clip **only the `native_refine_far` pair is filled**.
  The `native_refine` pair holds just a temperature; the other 20 are
  empty. So there's no slot to choose on this body.
- 📖 Each `DewarpParams`: `fx fy cx cy`, `width height`, `yaw pitch roll`,
  `k1…k9`, `xi`, tangential `p` / `tangent_coeff`, `lens_model`, an occlusion
  polygon (`occlusion_pt_x/y`: the body in the picture), temperature
  compensation, `cam_extri_q` (pose between the two camera modules) and
  `cam_imu_extri_q` (**camera-to-IMU rotation**).
- ✅ What the Avata 360 clip fills in: `width` / `height` 3840 (the
  sensor, twice the 1920 stream: every pixel value halves), `fx fy cx cy`,
  `k1…k5` (non-zero), `lens_model` 8, `yaw pitch roll`, `cam_imu_extri_q`,
  temperature compensation (enabled, `k` 6.65e-5) and `gimbal_yaw_h1/h2`
  (48 / 49, ❓). `xi`, `p`, `tangent_coeff`, `q` and the occlusion polygon
  are zero; there's no `cam_extri_q`.
- 📖 The lens model telemetry-parser reads is an **equidistant θ-polynomial**
  (Kannala-Brandt) with a tangential pair:

  ```text
  θ_d = θ·(1 + k1θ² + k2θ⁴ + k3θ⁶ + k4θ⁸ + k5θ¹⁰)
  x' = θ_d·X/ρ,  y' = θ_d·Y/ρ
  xd = x' + 2p1·x'y' + p2(θ_d² + 2x'²),   yd = y' + p1(θ_d² + 2y'²) + 2p2·x'y'
  u  = fx·xd + cx,  v = fy·yd + cy
  ```

  ✅ With the Avata 360's values this model, in image coordinates with x
  right and y down, maps both lenses so that their pictures match in the
  overlap: in a seam band 88-92° off-axis, 96 % of 10° sectors match at a
  median correlation of 0.92 once shifted by at most ~1° (16 frames). `xi = 0` here means the model has no
  `xi` term; it isn't an MEI lens with `xi = 0`. The app's stitch
  implements MEI only (X5 §5.3), so this needs a second model.
- 📖 `yaw pitch roll` and `cam_extri_q` describe the same pose (the
  quaternion is the triple composed `Ry(yaw)·Rz(roll)·Rx(pitch)`), camera
  from rig, in a frame whose up is `z` (pitch ≈ 90° is a level lens).
- ✅ On the Avata 360 clip: slave `yaw −179.12 pitch 90.09 roll 0.25`,
  master `−0.07 / 90.08 / 0.86`, so the two optical axes are **179.04°
  apart**. Read as camera-from-rig `Ry(yaw)·Rz(roll)·Rx(pitch − 90)`, the
  file's angles measurably improve the seam over an exact back-to-back
  pair: the roll takes out a 0.6° twist, and the yaw's 0.96° deviation
  more than halves the tilt between the lenses. Other rotation orders,
  sign changes and mirrored image axes all match worse.
- ❓ **Which lens is on which track** isn't settled on the Avata 360 clip.
  With the slave on track 0 the seam is closer (correlation at zero shift
  0.17 against 0.15, residual 0.35° radial and 0.79° tangential, once
  around the seam). With the master on track 0, a 1.27° tilt is left that
  a rotation alone would remove. Both residuals are the same in every
  frame, so they're geometry, not parallax. A tilt that size, or a ~12 px
  centre error, is more than a factory calibration should leave, so part
  of the reading is still missing. The image circle's centre doesn't
  decide it: the lens housing's glare bounds the picture, and on track 0
  the circle runs off the frame's sides.
- ✅ Left after the pose, in both readings: a constant 0.2-0.3° radial
  offset that changes from frame to frame with the scene. It's parallax
  between the two lenses' centres, which sit a few centimetres apart along
  the axis.
- 📖 No lens translation is stated: the stitching distance is baked into the
  "far" ladder of focal lengths instead.
- **Ours to measure** (see [`README.md`](README.md)): which of the 24 slots to
  stitch with by default and per accessory, the blend and seam widths, and
  how far off-axis each lens's picture is usable. telemetry-parser's values
  for these come from DJI Studio and aren't used.

## 4. IMU

- 📖 On the Osmo 360: raw gyro (rad/s) and accelerometer (g), stated at
  1000 Hz, plus DJI's own fused attitude quaternion per reading, packed
  per video frame.
- ✅ The Avata 360 clip has **only the fused attitude**: no raw gyro or
  accelerometer (`IMU_raw_attitude` is absent). So for the app it's an
  orientation track, like the Antigravity A1's, not gyro samples.
- ✅ Per frame, `IMU_attitude_after_fusion.current_frame` holds a
  timestamp, a vsync count and **39-41 quaternions** (`w x y z`): 4 kHz,
  as `imu_sampling_rate` says. Its timestamp sits 370-400 µs before the
  frame's `frame_timestamp`; frame timestamps step 10000 µs (9983 at the
  least).
- ✅ Each lens's `cam_imu_extri_q` on the Avata 360: about a half turn
  about `z` for the slave (w 0.007, z −1.000), about identity for the
  master (w 1.000, z 0.011).
- ✅ The Avata 360 flies its camera with the lens axes vertical: on our
  clip track 0 sees the sky and track 1 the ground while the drone is
  near level (−0.5° pitch, 1.9° roll by `drone_frame_meta`).
- 📖 Timing isn't exact from the stamps: the Osmo 360 II delivers a reading
  every ~995 µs, packets carry a fixed-offset stamp while the reading count
  drifts, and the IMU clock wanders against the frame clock (5 ms over
  5 minutes on an Osmo 360). telemetry-parser fits a straight line of
  anchor against reading index over neighbouring packets.
- 📖 Raw axes: `zyx` relative to the fused attitude in a 360 recording
  (Gyroflow's orientation notation, X5 §6.3).
- 📖 Rolling shutter: from `LRO_value` (ns per sensor line) × sensor height,
  18.3 ms on the Osmo 360; the generic `sensor_readout_time` (24.0 ms) is not
  what DJI's 360 stabilization uses.

## 5. Open questions

1. Which lens is on which track (§3), and the rest of the pose reading
   that leaves ~1° at the seam.
2. The attitude: its axes against the picture, its timing against the
   frames, and how `cam_imu_extri_q` enters.
3. The Avata 360's other modes, and everything above on the Osmo 360
   family: tracks, sizes, codec, the schema per product, the calibration
   slots and the raw IMU.
4. D-Log M's transfer curve and the colour tagging of 10-bit standard clips.
5. Which calibration slot stitches best, per accessory, where a body fills
   more than one (measured on our footage).
